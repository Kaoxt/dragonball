Your character, one level at a time

Your Main Personality (MP) is a stack of character levels. The active level shows your power stages, Power Up Rating and personality power.

  1. 1

    Name & level

    The name identifies your character. LEVEL is the position in your MP stack; start at level 1.

  2. 2

    Power stages

    Track your current stage on this scale. The number at that stage is your power rating, used for the Physical Attack Table.

  3. 3

    Power Up Rating (PUR)

    The printed PUR is how many stages your MP normally gains during Power Up, before bonuses.

  4. 4

    Alignment

    Hero, Villain or Rogue identifies the personality’s alignment and helps determine eligible Allies.

  5. 5

    Personality power

    Read the power box for attacks, effects, costs and timing. This Goku has a physical attack for 4 stages.

  6. 6

    Set & card number

    The footer identifies this printing. The number 179 is the card’s collector number, not a gameplay value.

In play

Goku’s printed PUR is 1, so he normally gains one stage during Power Up. His printed attack deals 4 stages before modifiers; it does not use the Attack Table.

What about Allies? Eligible personality cards can also support your MP as Allies. They enter during Non-Combat and follow the Ally guide.

Personality levels & anger

Allies

Eligible personality cards can support your Main Personality as Allies. Play them during Non-Combat to help absorb stage damage or take control of Combat when allowed.

  1. 1

    Name & level

    When first played, an Ally’s level cannot exceed your current MP level. An Ally cannot be the same character as either player’s MP.

  2. 2

    Power stages

    An Ally normally enters three stages above zero. It can receive an attack’s stage damage even when it is not controlling Combat.

  3. 3

    Power Up Rating (PUR)

    In the core Z guide, each Ally gains one stage during Power Up. The Expanded reference uses the printed PUR instead.

  4. 4

    Alignment

    This Krillin is a Hero personality. Check your MP’s alignment and your chosen format’s Ally eligibility rules.

  5. 5

    Personality power

    An eligible Ally can take control when your MP is at zero or one stage above zero. It may then use its personality power.

  6. 6

    Set & card number

    The footer identifies this Krillin as Saiyan Saga card 167. It is a standard personality card used in the Ally role.

In play

When this Krillin controls Combat, his printed energy attack costs 2 stages and deals 2 life cards of damage before modifiers.

Redirect an attack’s stage damage to one personality; do not split it between several Allies. Anger effects still affect your MP.

Mastery

A Mastery supports your deck’s declared Tokui-Waza. Use at most one, meet its style requirements, and place it in play before the game begins.

  1. 1

    Name & style

    This Mastery belongs to Red Style. Its style must match your qualifying Tokui-Waza.

  2. 2

    Card type

    Mastery starts and remains in play. It is not a card you draw and play from your hand during Combat.

  3. 3

    Power & frequency

    Read both the effect and its limits. This power is once per Combat and replaces an attack; it is not an extra free action.

  4. 4

    Set & card number

    The footer distinguishes this Mastery from other printings and versions.

In play

Red Style Mastery lets you banish the top card of your discard pile to raise anger by 1, or by 2 if that card is Red Style, once per Combat in place of an attack.

Declaring Tokui-Waza has deck-building requirements. Check those before choosing your Mastery.

Mastery & Tokui-Waza requirements

Sensei

A Sensei provides a power and sets the capacity of an optional Sensei Deck. You can use a Sensei even when its Sensei Deck contains no cards.

  1. 1

    Sensei power

    Read the printed power and its timing. It functions from the Sensei’s starting position in play.

  2. 2

    Sensei Deck capacity

    The printed capacity is a maximum, not a requirement. The Sensei Deck is a separate zone from your Life Deck, discard pile and removed-from-game cards.

  3. 3

    Pregame swaps

    Before play, reveal the cards you choose to swap in, exchange the same number from the top of your Life Deck, then shuffle the chosen cards into the Life Deck.

In play

A Sensei with capacity 6 allows up to six cards in its Sensei Deck. This number does not mean you draw six cards.

Use your chosen format’s Sensei Deck eligibility rules. Core Z and Expanded can differ.

Combat actions & defenses

Setups

Place a Setup face up during your Non-Combat step. It waits in play until you use its power at the allowed time, then is normally discarded.

  1. 1

    Card name

    Use the title to identify the Setup. A title without a martial arts style is normally Freestyle.

  2. 2

    Card type

    Setup is the site’s name for an ordinary Non-Combat card. Playing it and using its power are separate actions.

  3. 3

    Power box

    POWER introduces the effect you activate. Follow every instruction in order, including limits such as a personality’s highest stage.

  4. 4

    Set & card number

    The collector number identifies the card, not the amount of power it provides.

In play

When you use Power Up!, one of your personalities gains up to 5 stages, then you put the top card of your discard pile on the bottom of your Life Deck.

Unless the text says otherwise, discard the Setup after using it. Do not confuse a Setup card with the Power Up step of a turn.

Three paths to victory

★

Survival

Your opponent loses when their Life Deck empties. They also lose if unresolved damage remains and their deck contains only Dragon Balls.

★★

Dragon Balls

Control all seven of one type. Playing the seventh wins immediately; capturing it requires holding all seven until the start of your next turn.

★★★

Personality

Advance your Main Personality to the highest MP level available in this game. If your opponent has a higher maximum level, your lower maximum cannot win this way.

Prepare the table

  • Set out three to five consecutive levels of your Main Personality (MP), with level 1 active. Announce your highest level and declare a hero or villain alignment if your MP is a Rogue.
  • Place your optional Sensei and Mastery in their starting slots. Declare your qualifying Tokui-Waza. Set anger to zero and your MP to five power stages above zero.
  • Check Double Power using the power ratings at those starting stages. If one is at least twice the other, the stronger MP starts two stages above zero, the weaker starts at maximum stages, and the weaker player goes first. This rule does not apply to Z power ratings.
  • Shuffle your Life Deck; your opponent may cut. Resolve optional Sensei swaps as one batch, then shuffle again and offer another cut.
  • Unless Double Power determined the first player, the hero goes first. With matching alignments, use rock-paper-scissors. Do not deal an opening hand: the first active player draws during the Draw step.
Starter and High-Tech cards

Remove Battle Simulator-only cards. A villain starter, or a deck using Kid Trunks as MP, removes the starter’s Kid Trunks Ally. Choose the standard or High-Tech version of each MP level, not both. A multi-level High-Tech card requires using all levels printed on it.

Use Load deck for your starting cards. The MP, Mastery and Sensei slots run left to right. Resolve Double Power and choose the first player manually.

Seven steps. One turn

  1. Draw

    The active player draws three cards from the top of their Life Deck.

  2. Non-Combat

    The active player may put Allies, Dragon Balls, Non-Combat cards, Drills, Battlegrounds and Locations into play.

  3. Power Up

    Increase MP stages by its Power Up Rating (PUR), plus one for a declared Tokui-Waza. Each Ally gains one stage regardless of PUR. Never exceed maximum stages.

  4. Declare

    Choose whether to enter Combat. Playing a Battleground or Location this turn requires skipping Combat.

  5. Combat

    Resolve entering-Combat effects for the active player, then the opposing player. The opponent draws three; players alternate actions until both pass consecutively.

  6. Discard

    The active player discards down to zero or one card, then the opponent does the same.

  7. Rejuvenation

    If the active player did not declare Combat, they may move their top discard face down to the bottom of their Life Deck. Then the turn changes hands.

The table’s Next step indicator guides the turn. Players still resolve card effects and exceptions.

Combat, one action at a time

The active player remains the active player for the whole turn. Attacker and defender are roles that switch after each action. After both players resolve entering-Combat effects and the opponent draws three, the active player takes the first action.

What can I do on my action?

Perform an attack; activate an eligible effect instead of attacking; or pass. Attacks can come from hand cards, an MP power, or the Ally controlling Combat. Other actions can use eligible personality, Non-Combat, location or Sensei powers. An action without an attack skips the defense phase.

How can I defend?

When attacked, play or activate an eligible card or power that stops the attack or prevents its damage, or do nothing. A card that only ends Combat cannot be used as a defense unless it also directly stops or prevents an attack. An unstopped attack counts as successful even if its damage is zero.

Passing and ending Combat

A normal pass does not prevent you from acting later. Combat ends when both players pass back to back, or an applicable effect ends it.

Final Physical Attack

Once per Combat, discard any hand card to perform a physical attack using the Physical Attack Table. Afterward, you must pass for the rest of Combat and cannot use normal defenses. Existing Defense Shields and floating effects may still stop attacks.

5

The battle sequence

Use this order for each attack. Secondary effects happen before the opponent responds; costs, attacks, blocks, Endurance and successful-hit effects are separate parts of the sequence.

  1. Choose a card or power for your action. A pass skips to the exchange of roles.
  2. Pay its costs and declare any Empower choice.
  3. Resolve the attacker’s secondary effects.
  4. The defender chooses the personality controlling Combat, including a legal Ally takeover.
  5. The defender plays or activates an eligible defense.
  6. Resolve the defense’s secondary effects.
  7. If the attack remains unstopped, activate applicable Defense Shields.
  8. An attack still unstopped is successful, even with zero damage.
  9. Find base damage from the Physical Attack Table or the attack’s stated damage.
  10. Apply damage modifiers from cards and powers.
  11. If an eligible attacking Ally can make a personality capture, choose between that capture and life-card damage.
  12. Apply power-stage damage; stages beyond zero become life-card damage.
  13. Resolve life-card damage one card at a time, checking Dragon Balls and eligible Endurance.
  14. If at least five life cards were actually discarded as attack damage, the attacker may capture one Dragon Ball.
  15. Resolve the attacker’s successful-hit effects in the attacker’s chosen order. Then exchange attacker and defender roles.

Energy attacks, costs and overflow

An ordinary energy attack costs two stages and deals four life cards unless its text changes those values. Pay first; a stopped attack does not refund its cost. You cannot perform an attack whose cost you cannot pay. Energy Combat cards that are not energy attacks have no automatic two-stage cost.

For stage damage, lower the receiving personality’s stages to a minimum of zero, then take one life card for every unpaid stage. Life damage comes from the Life Deck, including when an Ally receives damage. For example, four stage damage against two remaining stages causes two stages lost and two life cards of damage.

Anger & personality levels

Anger cannot go below zero. At five or more, advance one MP level, reset anger to zero without carrying over excess, set stages to maximum and discard your Drills. Only your current MP level is in play.

Powers and losing a level

Personality powers normally work once per turn during Combat. Each actual level change makes the current MP power available again, even on returning to a previously used level. A constant Combat power must apply while that personality controls Combat.

A card effect can lower your MP level. Keep your current anger, set the lower level to five stages above zero, and discard your Drills.

Already at your highest level?

If your maximum level cannot win Personality victory, reaching five anger there instead resets anger and maximizes stages. This does not change levels, discard Drills, or refresh the personality power.

Endurance, shields & focused attacks

Endurance

When a card is revealed as attack damage from your Life Deck, you may use its Endurance to prevent that many remaining life cards of damage. The revealed card itself has already counted as damage; remove it from the game if you use its Endurance. Unused prevention does not carry forward.

You must have declared Tokui-Waza to use Endurance. It cannot come from your hand, cards in play or earlier discards. It cannot prevent non-attack life loss or damage that cannot be prevented. It can apply even if the attack removes damaged cards from the game. Endurance X uses the condition printed on that card. Reducing actual damage below five can prevent a life-card Dragon Ball capture.

Defense Shields

A matching shield automatically activates when you have not stopped an attack. It stops that attack; if several shields are available, choose their activation order. Focused attacks and attacks whose damage cannot be prevented activate all matching shields and pass through them.

Focused and unstoppable attacks

A focused attack cannot be stopped by Defense Shields, Masteries or a card that stops both physical and energy attacks. An appropriate defense that only stops that one attack type can stop it. You may play an otherwise eligible defense for its secondary effects even if it cannot stop the focused or unstoppable attack.

Allies & control of Combat

Play an Ally during your Non-Combat step at three stages above zero. Its level must be no higher than your current MP level. Later, overlay its next consecutive level and set it to maximum stages; only the top level remains in play. An overlay can exceed your current MP level.

Taking over and using powers

An Ally may take control when your MP is at zero or one stage above zero. Its rating then determines attack-table damage, and it may use its power even if the MP already used theirs. Choose the defending personality before resolving a defense; keep the acting personality in control until that attack finishes.

When an opponent plays or uses a card outside their defense phase, an eligible Ally may take control before its effects. Another Ally or the MP can later take over without waiting for the current Ally to reach low stages. Allies cannot use entering-Combat powers. Constant powers apply only while controlling Combat. Anger effects always affect the MP.

Absorbing damage

An Ally can receive stage damage for another personality even without controlling Combat. Calculate damage using the personalities actually in control, then route it. One recipient takes the entire attack’s stage damage; you cannot split it between Allies. Overflow still damages your Life Deck.

Duplicate Allies and alignment

An Ally cannot be the same character as either player’s MP. Both players can have the same Ally character at different levels, but normally no duplicate character and level may be in play. Allies match their MP’s hero/villain alignment; Majin MPs and Majin Allies must be paired. Rogue Allies inherit the MP’s alignment.

Rogues in this edition: Android 16, Android 18, Piccolo, Vegeta and Majin Vegeta. See the special personality rules for Saibaimen and Cell Jr. exceptions.

Card types, Drills & locations

A title beginning with Red, Blue, Orange, Black, Saiyan or Namekian identifies a styled card. Other cards are Freestyle. A character name makes a card Named; this does not by itself restrict who may use it. Follow any explicit character-only restriction.

Combat and Non-Combat cards

Physical Combat, Energy Combat and Event (the original standalone Combat type) are distinct card types. They are generally played from your hand during Combat for their stated purpose. Place Setups (regular Non-Combat cards) in your Non-Combat step, activate them when eligible, and discard after use. Dragon Balls follow their own rules even if printed with a Non-Combat heading.

Drills

Play Drills during Non-Combat. They stay in play after use; most have mandatory ongoing effects. Your styled Drills must all share one style, with no duplicate copy of a styled Drill in play. Freestyle and Named Drills can coexist with styled Drills and can have multiple copies within deck limits.

Discard your Drills whenever your MP actually gains or loses a level. When you draw a Drill incompatible with the style of your existing Drills, you may immediately reveal it and shuffle it into your Life Deck, without a replacement draw. A Drill’s added damage applies even if the attack did not originally deal that damage type.

Restricted Drills

A Drill that prohibits other Drills of its stated style cannot enter while one is anywhere in play. If another Drill of that style enters afterward, discard the restricted Drill. If the same restricted Drill enters, discard both.

Battlegrounds and Locations

Play these during Non-Combat, then skip Combat for that turn. You can still rejuvenate. Only one Battleground or Location is in play across the table: a new one removes the previous one from the game. You cannot play another copy of the same one already in play.

Dragon Balls & captures

Your deck may contain only one type: Earth, Namek or Dende, with at most one of each number. Alternate Dende versions count as the same numbered ball. A duplicate cannot be in play anywhere on the table. Play them during Non-Combat and resolve their powers as instructed.

Dragon Balls leaving the Life Deck

A Dragon Ball headed for the discard pile bypasses it: remove it from the game if that ball is already in play; otherwise place it face down at the bottom of your Life Deck. During attack damage it does not count as a damaged card, so continue revealing. For non-attack life-card loss it does count toward the required loss. If damage remains and your deck contains only Dragon Balls, you lose.

Three ways to capture
  • Life-card capture: after a successful attack actually discards at least five life cards of damage, take one opposing Dragon Ball in play.
  • Personality capture: an attacking Ally from the following list can exchange at least one life card of resulting damage for a capture: Bulma, Chi-Chi, Frieza, Garlic Jr., Guldo, Krillin, Master Roshi, Saibaimen, Videl, Tien or Yamcha. The attack must succeed; this replaces life-card damage.
  • Card capture: follow a card effect that permits a capture.

You may capture a different type from your own deck’s type. At capture, choose all or none of the ball’s powers. A captured ball’s previous floating effect ends and is granted to its new controller only if they choose its power. Ownership never changes.

Winning and immunity

Placing your seventh matching ball into play wins immediately. Capturing your seventh wins only if you still hold all seven at the beginning of your next turn.

Effects must specifically mention Dragon Balls to affect them. Random effects may still reposition them inside a deck or discard them from a hand. A generic search for a card cannot select a Dragon Ball.

Sensei, Mastery & Tokui-Waza

Sensei Deck and setup swaps

A Sensei may be used for its power without a Sensei Deck. To have a Sensei Deck, include a Sensei and stay within its printed capacity. Starting contents must be Fusion personalities or cards marked for a Sensei Deck. Fusion personalities count toward capacity but cannot be swapped into the Life Deck at setup.

Choose and reveal all cards to swap first. Set those aside, move the same number of original top Life Deck cards into the Sensei Deck, then shuffle the chosen cards into the Life Deck. Offer a cut afterward. You can inspect your own Sensei Deck at any time; your opponent cannot. Its cards are outside play, but are not removed from the game.

Mastery and style requirements

Use at most one Mastery. Its style must match a qualifying declared Tokui-Waza. It starts in play, counts toward deck size and remains in play. Its effects originate from the Mastery itself.

For styled Tokui-Waza, all styled cards in the deck must share one style, with at least one styled card beyond the Mastery. Freestyle cards may be included. Your Sensei Deck must also match the style or be Freestyle. Freestyle Tokui-Waza and its Mastery require no styled cards. Declaring a qualifying Tokui-Waza gives the MP +1 PUR and enables Endurance.

Saiyan and Namekian eligibility

Saiyan Tokui-Waza MPs: Bardock, Broly, Cell, Future Gohan, Gohan, Goku, Goten, Gotenks, Kid Trunks, Majin Vegeta, Mighty Mask, Nappa, Raditz, Trunks and Vegeta.

Namekian Tokui-Waza MPs: Cell, Dende, Lord Slug, Nail, Piccolo and Pikkon. Pikkon may declare this style but cannot use Namekian Heritage-only cards. Namekian Tokui-Waza permits a 90-card deck.

Build a legal deck

  • Size: 50–85 cards, or up to 90 with Namekian Tokui-Waza. Count all MP levels, Mastery and the Sensei card; do not count the separate Sensei Deck.
  • MP stack: three to five consecutive levels of one personality, starting at 1. You may mix sagas, but cannot mix characters treated as distinct personalities.
  • Copies: normally up to three of a Physical Combat, Energy Combat, Combat, Non-Combat or Battleground/Location card. Your MP’s matching Named cards allow four. Obey stricter printed limits.
  • Allies: at least two levels below the highest MP level in your deck. Normally one of each exact Ally version, though different versions at the same level may be included. No High-Tech foil Allies, no Ally of your MP character, and obey alignment rules.
  • Starting cards: at most one Mastery and one Sensei. One Dragon Ball type with no repeated numbers.
  • Style: check both Life Deck and Sensei Deck against your declared Tokui-Waza. Check Sensei capacity and starting-card eligibility.

Tournament banned/restricted lists and later errata are format-specific. This guide preserves the original Score DBZ baseline; agree on any later format changes before playing. The table’s import warnings are not a full legality check.

Timing & special keywords

Secondary effects, choices and simultaneous effects

Resolve secondary effects before the opponent’s response, even if an attack is later stopped. A successful-hit effect waits until after damage and captures. If effects offer an “or” choice, choose one; otherwise resolve the effects in written order within the battle sequence. When both players would resolve effects together, the active player resolves theirs first, then the opponent.

Use when needed and floating effects

Use-when-needed effects can happen between the fifteen battle-sequence parts, not in the middle of one. If an effect affects that card, finish the effect before using the card. Outside Combat, use them when needed.

A duration-based floating effect lasts until its specified endpoint even after its source card is discarded. When that is the card’s final effect, discard the card after creating it, except personality cards.

Empower

Declare Empower when paying for an attack. It adds its number as life-card damage while ignoring all card text after the Empower instruction for that attack. Read the whole card before choosing.

Searches, revealed cards and cards underneath

When selecting cards from your Life Deck, show the chosen cards and shuffle afterward. Revealed cards without a destination instruction return to their original location in their original order. Face-down cards stored beneath another card are discarded when that parent leaves play unless its text says otherwise. Italic explanatory parentheses are outside ordinary effect timing.

Fusion & special personalities

Fusion and the five-turn timer

Use an effect allowing two matching-level, fusible personalities to fuse. Take the corresponding Fusion personality of that level from your Sensei Deck. It replaces the pair as one personality, at maximum stages and zero anger. An MP fusion becomes your MP; an Ally-only fusion is one Ally. Component identities still count for applicable game effects and character-specific targeting.

At the start of each of your turns, place your top Life Deck card beneath the anger counter as a timer. At five or more cards, immediately defuse: remove the Fusion personality from the game, return both component personalities at maximum stages, and shuffle the timer cards into the Life Deck. A Fusion may use either component’s character-only cards.

Separate personalities and heritage

Vegeta and Majin Vegeta, Trunks and Kid Trunks, and Gohan and Future Gohan are separate characters. Do not combine their MP levels or share their character-only permissions. Named cards still allow the normal three copies unless they match your MP.

Saiyan Heritage: Broly, Cell, Cell Jr. 1 and 2, Future Gohan, Gohan, Goku, Goten, Gotenks, Kid Trunks, Majin Vegeta, Mighty Mask, Nappa, Paragus, Raditz, Trunks and Vegeta. Older cards restricted to villains, Goku and Gohan use this heritage rule.

Namekian Heritage: Cell, Cell Jr. 1 and 2, Dende, Lord Slug, Nail and Piccolo. Heritage eligibility and Tokui-Waza eligibility are different lists.

Saibaimen and Cell Jr.

Saibaimen permit duplicate Allies: up to three copies of each card in a deck and nine across the field, with three at each level. If Saibaimen is an MP, no Saibaimen Allies are allowed.

Cell Jr. Allies require a Cell MP and allow duplicates, with up to nine across the field. Cell Jr. 1 and Cell Jr. 2 are distinct; overlay the matching character’s next level. The later clarification in this rulebook says Cell Jr. cards are not Cell’s Named cards, so they do not receive the four-copy Named allowance.

Ginyu Force, Mighty Mask and Majin Yakon

Ginyu Force as MP counts as four Allies for effects, but those counted Allies cannot absorb redirected damage. As an Ally it counts as one personality. With a Ginyu Force MP, its individual members cannot appear as Allies, and member cards cannot form its MP stack. Ginyu Force is villain-aligned.

Mighty Mask similarly counts as one Ally for game effects. It is distinct from Goten and Trunks, can declare Saiyan Tokui-Waza and use Saiyan Heritage, Goten-only and Kid Trunks-only cards, but cannot fuse into Gotenks.

Majin Yakon’s X PUR copies the opposing MP’s current printed PUR. Against another Majin Yakon, both have PUR 1. In multiplayer, choose which opponent’s MP to copy.

How to use this site

Move cards with a mouse or your finger

Drag one of your cards onto your play area. Drop it where you want it to stay. On a phone or tablet, drag with one finger; move near the upper or lower screen edge to scroll. The other player receives the position when you drop.

You can also tap a card, choose a destination in the Card Inspector, and select Move card.

Inspect, flip and mark a card used

Select any visible card to inspect it. For your cards in play, use Flip to change which side your opponent sees, or Mark used as a reminder. You can still see the identity of your own face-down cards.

Manage the Life Deck and hand

Draw 3 and Draw 1 move cards into your private hand. Search Life Deck opens your private search panel; finish with Finish search & shuffle. The opponent sees that you are searching, but cannot see the search results.

Adjust personality level, anger and stages

Use the plus and minus buttons beside your counters. Changing the MP level does not automatically resolve every associated rule: make the other required adjustments yourself. Ally stages are available through the Card Inspector.

Practice or invite a friend

Solo practice lets you switch between both seats. In a private room, Copy invite gives you a link for your opponent. Both players load decks before the host starts setup.

Resolve a disagreement

Use the game log and chat to explain the action, compare the relevant card text, and agree on the correction before continuing. This arena helps track the game; it does not adjudicate every card interaction.

Quick definitions for the terms used throughout this guide.

MP

PUR

Your draw pile and the cards lost when taking life damage.
